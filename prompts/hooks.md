## Hook types (pick the one that fits the take; the examples are the reference creator's, register only, never reuse)
- bold_reframe: one sharp line that renames the thing. "India's real luxury market isn't fashion or cars. It's privacy." Fits a single crisp insight.
- doesnt_add_up: a fact or number that shouldn't be true, then a hint it means something. "Dhurandhar 2 is almost 4 hours long. And people are sitting through every single minute of it." Fits a surprising number or event.
- sounds_wrong: a claim that sounds absurd, owned up front. "There's a generation in India earning 30 LPA that genuinely feels poor. And I know how that sounds." Fits a counterintuitive take.
- motive_bust: the reason everyone gives, then the real one. "People don't move to Bangalore just for the job. They move to become someone their hometown wouldn't recognize." Fits the real reason behind a behaviour.
- weird_behaviour: name a contradiction in how people act. "There's something bizarre about Indian consumer behaviour." Fits spending and habits that don't match.
- before_after: how fast something flipped. "India went from 'therapy is for crazy people' to 'therapy is cool' in about four years." Fits a fast shift.
- imagine: a picture that makes a big idea concrete. "Imagine you're in a massive auditorium with 362 million Indians." Fits a scale or systems idea.
- prediction: where this is heading, said plainly. "I have a prediction. In a year or two..." Fits a trend.
- rant: an exasperated opener. "Enough. I don't want to hear another word about AI." Fits the rant mood.
- satire_list: a list setup. "Literally everyone around me is:" Fits the funny mood, single slides.
- open_letter: "Dear ___," Fits addressing a group directly.
- question_gap: a concrete contradiction, then the question the post answers. "India's best-selling phone costs more than most Indians earn in a month. So how is everyone affording it?" Fits a mystery the post solves.
- vivid_reframe: what people actually did, said so plainly it surprises, plus a bigger stake. "Nobody bought an iPhone 17 this Diwali. They bought twelve payments of ₹8,667. That's a much bigger story than Apple." Fits a behaviour hiding in plain sight.
